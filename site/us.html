US agent payments, governed — two different rules, not one.

An unvalidated bank account is a Nacha system problem — a flat refusal, not a question for a human. A missing consumer authorization is a Regulation E problem — exactly the kind of decision only the consumer can make. SpendVeto keeps them separate, the way the rules actually do.

What's real, with sources

Nacha WEB debit account validation (effective 19 March 2021): the FIRST use of a payee's account number, or any change to it, must be validated (prenotification, micro-deposit, or a validation service) before it's debited — a system control for fraud detection, not a consumer-authorization step. Same Day ACH entries are additionally capped at USD 1,000,000 per transaction, unconditionally.

Sources: Nacha — WEB debit account validation rule

Regulation E (12 CFR 1005, implementing the EFTA): a preauthorized recurring debit needs the consumer's written (or similarly authenticated) authorization on file; a varying debit amount needs at least 10 days' notice; a consumer's oral stop-payment order is immediately binding and lapses 14 days after the request unless confirmed in writing.

Sources: Federal Reserve — Regulation E compliance guide

Try the rules

This runs SpendVeto's actual US evaluator in your browser — the same file the server uses. Change a debit and watch the verdict.

What this is not. SpendVeto does not originate, collect or settle any ACH entry, is not an ODFI/RDFI or payment processor, and claims no Nacha or Federal Reserve authorization. An unvalidated account is a hard DENY, not a pause — Nacha's rule is a system control, not a human-authorization gate. "Requires approval" is reserved for the consumer's own authorization and notice rights under Regulation E. Account numbers are masked in every decision (GLBA nonpublic personal information). Self-hosting keeps all data on your own infrastructure, in the region you choose.
