UK agent payments, governed — Confirmation of Payee, every time.

Faster Payments has no recurring-pull equivalent — an agent paying in the UK PUSHES money to a payee it names, once. That's exactly the fact pattern UK Authorised Push Payment fraud exists for. SpendVeto checks the Confirmation of Payee result and the reimbursement exposure before the agent can proceed, and an agent can never wave through a no-match on its own.

What's real, with sources

Confirmation of Payee (Payment Systems Regulator Specific Direction 17, successor to SD10): before a push payment settles, the sending PSP checks the payee name against the account and returns match, close_match, no_match, or unavailable. Proceeding past a no-match or unavailable result is the single most-cited UK APP fraud pattern.

Sources: UK Finance — Confirmation of Payee · PSR — Specific Direction 10/17

APP fraud reimbursement (PSR PS23/4 + PS24/4, in force 7 October 2024): mandatory reimbursement on Faster Payments and CHAPS, cap reduced to £85,000 (aligned to the FSCS limit) under the PSR's 2025 policy update, a £100 claim excess (waived for vulnerable customers), 50:50 cost split between sending and receiving PSPs. Above the cap, there is no reimbursement backstop at all.

Sources: Hogan Lovells — APP fraud reimbursement · City AM — reimbursement cap

Try the rules

This runs SpendVeto's actual UK evaluator in your browser — the same file the server uses. Change a payment and watch the verdict.

What this is not. SpendVeto does not initiate, collect or settle any Faster Payments transaction, is not a PSP, and claims no PSR/FCA authorisation. "Requires approval" means the payer makes the call themselves — on a Confirmation of Payee mismatch, a new payee, or exposure above the reimbursement cap, an agent cannot weigh that risk on the payer's behalf. Payee account numbers are masked in every decision (UK GDPR / DPA 2018). Self-hosting keeps all data on your own infrastructure, in the region you choose.
